Our Data Warehouse Migration Strategy

So, we have established that our data warehouse migration services support your transition from legacy systems to modern, cloud-native architectures.

But what strategies do we adopt to bring the desired results?

One of the major decisions lies in whether to convert your existing pipelines/code or rebuild them from scratch. The right strategy—convert or rebuild—depends on your performance goals and time-to-value expectations.

Here’s how we decide what’s best for your business:

Automatic/Tool-Assisted Conversion

If you have many pipelines to migrate and want to preserve as much of the current logic as possible, then it’s the better option. Some tools or accelerators can help translate SQL dialects, adapt ETL workflows, or convert code or metadata definitions.

Rebuild/Rewrite Pipelines

Rebuild is better when logic is complex, legacy, inefficient, or utilises non-portable components (UDFs, obscure dependencies). It enables the usage of Databricks-native features, including Delta Lake, Auto Loader, structured streaming, and Databricks SQL for cleaner, more maintainable pipelines.

Our Migration Approach: Phases We Undertake

Our team follows a time-tested, de-risked migration methodology designed for speed, accuracy, and zero data loss.

01

Assessment & Discovery

Our team evaluates your existing data warehouses, ETL pipelines, reporting systems, and storage to identify complexity, dependencies, and workloads. We also align your business priorities with technical requirements by choosing a high-level migration strategy.

02

Planning & Design

We then prepare a detailed migration roadmap, which includes selecting the target cloud platform (Snowflake, BigQuery, Azure Synapse, etc.), DWH architecture blueprints, migration sequencing, and cutover plans. Our experts identify migration accelerators, automation tools, and cloud services to speed up the process.

03

Schema & Code Conversion

Legacy database objects, such as tables, indexes, views, ETL scripts, and stored procedures, are converted to cloud-compatible equivalents. Automated tools may be used to handle SQL dialect translation and pipeline refactoring.

04

Data Migration & Validation

We begin migrating the actual data from the source to the target, which may be done in batches or via continuous replication. Our team conducts timely performance tests and reconciliation checks to confirm that migrated data matches source data and meets quality benchmarks.

05

Integration & Optimization

After successful data migration, integrations with BI tools, analytics platforms, and downstream applications are re-established. We fine-tune the workloads to leverage cloud-native advantages, such as partitioning, elastic compute, caching, and clustering. We focus primarily on query performance, workload balancing, and cost optimization in data warehouse migration.

06

Testing & Cutover

Extensive functional and performance testing ensures that dashboards and analytics are behaving as expected in the new environment. We also run parallel tests across legacy and cloud systems to compare results.

07

Monitoring & Continuous Improvement

Post-migration monitoring is essential for establishing consistent data quality and performance. Cost monitoring and governance controls are introduced to prevent overspending.

Our Engagement Models

When engaging with customers, Aegis Softtech offers multiple pricing and engagement approaches tailored to the nature, complexity, scope, and risk profile of each project.

The goal is to align commercial terms with client expectations and business outcomes.

Fixed-Price (Scope-Limited) Model

It offers a well-defined scope, timeline, and deliverables, all of which are agreed upon upfront, and the price is fixed accordingly. It’s best suited for short-term projects with clear requirements, well-documented migration tasks or repeatable implementations.

Time & Materials (T&M) Model

You pay based on the actual effort spent, which involves hourly/daily/monthly rates for consultants, engineers, and architects. It’s best for complex, evolving, or exploratory projects or scenarios where requirements are dynamic or not fully defined upfront.

Outcome-Based (SLA-Tied) Model

Pricing is linked to achieving stated business outcomes or service level targets, such as a set percentage of migration success, system uptime, or performance SLAs. It’s best for long-term partnerships where measurable outcomes are critical or clients who prefer to pay for results rather than effort.

Proof of Concept (PoC) Pricing

Chosen for a small-scale pilot engagement at a lower cost or fixed minimal pricing to validate feasibility before a full rollout. It’s suitable for those trying new technology adoption (e.g., Databricks migration pilot, Snowflake PoC) or seeking to validate ROI or technical compatibility before committing.

FAQs

Cloud-native data warehouse migration is the process of migrating your workloads and data from a traditional, on-premises data warehouse to a modern, cloud-based DWH designed to run on cloud infrastructure. It encompasses re-architecting the system to modernize and leverage cloud advantages, such as scalability and flexibility, by decoupling storage and compute.

Deciding between rehost, replatform, and refactor migration depends on your organization’s priorities for budget, speed, long-term benefits, and modernization.

Rehost is the fastest and most budget-friendly option if the application can be moved with minimal changes. Replatform helps maintain speed with minor data warehouse modernization without a full rewrite. Refactoring involves extensive code changes and is thus best when the goal is significant innovation and agility for the long term.

Hiring professionals for data warehouse migration is imperative for ensuring data integrity, mitigating risks, minimizing downtime, and cost and time efficiency.

The four main types of data migration are:

  • Storage migration: Moving data from one storage system to another for faster and more reliable performance.
  • Database migration: Transferring a database from one platform to another to upgrade the database or consolidate data.
  • Application migration: Moving data from one application to another or from one environment to a different one, especially when updating software.
  • Cloud migration: Moving data from on-premises systems to a cloud-based infrastructure or from one cloud provider to another.

The duration of a data warehouse migration can vary significantly depending on factors such as data volume, target platform, modernization level, and source complexity. We do offer a one-size-fits-all estimate and instead begin every engagement with a readiness assessment, scoping your workloads, and identifying dependencies.

We anticipate and mitigate the key issues commonly arising during migration. Some of which include:

  • Data loss or corruption
  • Unplanned downtime
  • Performance degradation
  • Security and compliance risks
  • Scope and cost overruns
